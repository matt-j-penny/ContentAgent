# Content Types & Value Delivery Outlines

Every short gets classified into exactly one type **before** writing. The type decides what
goes in the value delivery — the middle of the script. Without this the middle turns into
interchangeable filler, which is the single most common failure mode.

The spine is fixed for all types: **Hook → Open Loop → Value Delivery → Payoff.**
Only the value delivery changes.

**Across every type: one idea per script.** The outlines below are the shape of that one idea,
not a list of boxes to fill with separate facts. A beat that doesn't set up or pay off the single
point comes out, however interesting it is on its own.

**When a type genuinely carries more than one item, number them out loud** — the count goes in
the hook ("three new tricks"), the open loop reaches into the list ("and the third one works in
total silence"), and each item is spoken as "One…", "Two…", "Three…". Three maximum.

---

## 1. New model / new tool

**Use when:** something launched or shipped and the video is about the thing itself.

**Value delivery (≈15–25s):**

| Beat | Job |
|---|---|
| **What's actually new** | One sentence. Not what the tool *is* — what changed as of today. Assume they know the category |
| **2–3 standout capabilities** | The specific things it does that the previous version or the competitor doesn't. One line each, concrete, no adjectives doing the work |
| **What it beats or replaces** | Name the thing in their current stack this displaces. This is what makes it actionable rather than trivia |

**Rules:**
- Capabilities are demonstrated, not described. "It writes tests" is dead; "point it at a repo, it opens the PR" is alive.
- **Two or three capabilities means a numbered list.** Put the count in the hook, point the open loop at one item, and say "One… Two… Three…" on the lines themselves. An unnumbered run of capabilities is the exact point where this type turns into a feature list read aloud.
- Never list more than three. Four capabilities and none of them land.
- **Describe behaviour, never the internal name.** "It understands what you're looking at" beats "upgraded to the Muse Spark models" — the codename costs a beat to explain and teaches the viewer nothing. Version numbers and model families go in the caption.
- If you can't name what it replaces, the video has no stakes — reconsider the idea.

**Fails when:** it becomes a feature list read aloud, or it explains what the category is to people who already know.

---

## 2. News story

**Use when:** an event happened. Launches sit in type 1; this is everything else — funding, lawsuits, breaches, policy, drama.

**Value delivery (≈15–25s):**

| Beat | Job |
|---|---|
| **The key facts** | Who, what, and the one number that matters. Tight — this is the part people came for |
| **Brief context** | The minimum background that makes the facts mean something. One or two lines, no history lesson |
| **Why it matters to them** | The consequence for the viewer specifically. Not "this is a big deal for the industry" |

**Rules:**
- One number, not five. Pick the number that carries the story and drop the rest.
- Context comes *after* facts, never before. Leading with background is the classic retention killer.
- If the "why it matters" is speculative, say so plainly rather than dressing a guess as a conclusion.

**Fails when:** it's a summary with no consequence, or the consequence is vague industry-level hand-waving.

---

## 3. Tutorial / process

**Use when:** the viewer should be able to do the thing after watching.

**Value delivery (≈20–30s):**

| Beat | Job |
|---|---|
| **The steps in order** | One line each. Numbered out loud. Three to five steps maximum for short-form |
| **The gotcha** | The place most people break it. This is the beat that earns the save — deliver it as its own moment, not as an aside |

**Rules:**
- Steps must be genuinely sequential. If order doesn't matter it's a listicle, not a tutorial — reclassify.
- Screen or hands on every step; a talking head narrating steps has no reason to be video.
- The gotcha goes near the end, never buried in step two.

**Fails when:** there are seven steps (nobody retains them), or the gotcha is missing and it reads like documentation.

---

## 4. Opinion / take

**Use when:** the value is your judgement, not the information.

**Value delivery (≈15–25s):**

| Beat | Job |
|---|---|
| **The claim** | Stated flatly, as a truth, in one sentence. No hedging, no "I think" |
| **The evidence** | The specific thing that made you believe it — a number, a case, something you ran yourself |
| **The pre-empted objection** | Name the strongest counter-argument out loud and answer it. This is what separates a take from a rant |

**Rules:**
- Evidence must be first-hand or specific. "Everyone's saying" is not evidence.
- The objection beat is mandatory. Skipping it hands the comments to whoever raises it first.
- Contrarian only when you actually believe it; manufactured contrarianism reads instantly.

**Fails when:** the claim is safe enough that nobody would argue, or the evidence is a vibe.

---

## 5. Comparison

**Use when:** the viewer is choosing between options.

**Value delivery (≈20–30s):**

| Beat | Job |
|---|---|
| **The contenders** | Named fast, two or three maximum |
| **2–3 axes** | The dimensions that actually decide it — price, speed, where each breaks. Same axes applied to each contender |
| **The verdict** | Pick one. Say who each is for |

**Rules:**
- Same axes for every contender, in the same order — the symmetry is what makes it scannable.
- A verdict is mandatory. "It depends" is why nobody finishes comparison videos.
- Two contenders is usually better than three in short-form.

**Fails when:** it's balanced to the point of uselessness, or the axes are chosen to flatter a predetermined winner.

---

## Classification is a decision, not a guess

If a story spans two types, pick the one matching **why the viewer clicked**, and let the other
type's material live in the payoff. A model launch with a dramatic backstory is still type 1 —
the story is the reward, not the value delivery.

If nothing fits, say so and propose the outline you'd use instead rather than forcing it into the
nearest box.
