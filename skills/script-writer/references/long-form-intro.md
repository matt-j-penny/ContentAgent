# Long-form intro

Writes the first 20-40 seconds of a YouTube video: the part that decides whether
the click survives. More viewers retained in the first 30s means more viewers
watching the rest of the video — this is one of the highest-leverage things to
get right.

**Core premise:** the viewer just clicked on a title and thumbnail. The intro's
only job is to confirm they clicked the right video, then earn the next 10
minutes. Four beats, in order, no filler before beat one.

## The four beats

### 1. Payoff

The first line, before anything else. It repeats the promise of the title so
the viewer instantly knows they clicked the right video.

- Make it as broad and as extreme as you can get it **without lying**.
- No greeting, no channel intro, no "in this video" — the payoff line comes
  before any of that.
- If the title makes a promise, the payoff line restates that promise in a
  sharper, punchier form.

> "When you use this system, you'll never need to check your email again."
> "You may not even need to write another email — it's that good."

### 2. Social proof

Credentials that prove you know what you're talking about. Pick the most
concrete, most impressive fact you honestly have:

- How much money it's made, saved, or made your business
- How many hours you spent building/testing/refining it
- How long you've used it, or how many people have used it
- A result someone can independently verify

> "I use this system every day, and it's saved me hundreds of hours of work."

Generic proof ("I've studied this for years") is weaker than a specific number.
Always reach for the number.

### 3. Context

This is where the video's worth goes up. Name the price paid, the time it took,
or the thing nobody else has shown — whatever makes the payoff (beat 1) look
earned rather than lucky.

> "It's like having your own personal assistant that tells you what's high
> priority, responds for you, stops you from being charged for recurring
> subscriptions, and keeps you organized so you never have to worry about
> email again."

### 4. Setup

Name exactly what the viewer walks out with. This is the explicit "here's
what's coming" line that turns a vague promise into a concrete deliverable.

> "So today, I'm going to give you the 5 steps to set this up for yourself —
> and I'll show you one feature almost nobody knows about."

A strong setup line often has two parts: the main deliverable, plus one
specific teased detail (a bonus tip, a step most people skip, a number) to open
a loop that pulls the viewer into the body of the video.

## Rules

- **Payoff first, always.** No channel branding, no "hey guys welcome back",
  no logo animation before the payoff line lands.
- **Broad but honest.** Extreme framing is fine; false claims aren't. If beat 1
  overclaims, the viewer feels it by minute 2 and leaves — worse than a modest
  hook, because now they distrust the rest of the video too.
- **Specific beats vague, everywhere.** "Saved me hundreds of hours" beats "saved
  me a ton of time." "5 steps" beats "a few steps."
- **One idea, not a list of features.** The payoff is one sharp promise, not a
  summary of everything in the video — save the rest for context and setup.
- **Keep it fast.** Four beats in 20-40 seconds total. If any beat needs more
  than 2-3 sentences, it's carrying content that belongs later in the video.

## Pressure-test before delivering

- [ ] Payoff line comes before any greeting or branding
- [ ] Payoff matches the title's promise, pushed slightly further
- [ ] Payoff is extreme but not a lie
- [ ] Social proof is a specific, concrete credential — not a vague claim
- [ ] Context explains what makes the payoff earned (cost, time, exclusivity)
- [ ] Setup names the exact deliverable the viewer walks out with
- [ ] Setup teases one specific detail to open a loop into the body
- [ ] Whole intro reads in under ~40 seconds out loud
- [ ] Read aloud: does it sound like a person talking, or like written copy?

## Output format

1. The four beats labelled (Payoff / Social Proof / Context / Setup), each as
   spoken lines
2. The full intro as one continuous block, ready to read on camera
3. One alternative payoff line, in case the first is too aggressive for the claim
