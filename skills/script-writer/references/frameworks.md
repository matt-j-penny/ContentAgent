# Script Frameworks — optional narrative overlays

**These are not the default.** The default structure for any short is the spine in SKILL.md
(Hook → Open Loop → Value Delivery → Payoff), with the value delivery shaped by the content
type in `content-types.md`.

Reach for a framework below only when a piece is genuinely narrative — a story, a journey, a
build-up — and say why you chose it. Applied by default they produce interchangeable scripts,
because they specify beat *names* ("Body", "Show") without specifying what goes in them.

`Stop → Stack` is the exception: it's the hook engine and always applies.

---

## HRST — Hook → Relatable → Show → Takeaway
*Source: Inside Q (Instagram scriptwriting)*

The default for teaching, tips and value content. **Total runtime 30–45s.**

| Beat | Seconds | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–3 | Stop the scroll, promise the payoff |
| **Relatable** | 3–10 | Hit a lived experience — "you've felt this" — so they see themselves in it |
| **Show** | 10–35 | Visual storytelling: demonstrate, don't describe. Build the mental picture |
| **Takeaway** | 35–45 | The lesson, framed as a reflection they'll think about after |

## Stop → Stack
*Source: vidIQ*

Not a full script — the hook engine that feeds every other framework. The first **1.5 seconds**
decides the video.

1. **Stop** — interrupt the autopilot scroll with a visual/audio pattern break
2. **Stack** — immediately add a statement introducing contrast, challenging a belief, or setting stakes

Getting the order wrong (meaning before interrupt) means nobody hears the meaning.

## Hook → Body → Reward
*Source: E'Calm (800M+ views on Shorts)*

Entertainment, payoff-driven and story shorts.

| Beat | Seconds | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–3 | Promise |
| **Body** | 3–~25 | Escalate toward the promise, no detours |
| **Reward** | last beat | The most outrageous / satisfying moment — then **cut instantly** |

The cut is the framework. Anything after the payoff is where they swipe.

## Intro → Research → Body → Outro (Retention Dance)
*Source: Tube Sensei*

Longer shorts and packaging-led videos where title/thumbnail carry the click.

- **Intro** — deliver **click confirmation within 3–6 seconds** (visually or verbally prove this is what was promised), then open a **curiosity gap** that gives a reason to stay to the end
- **Body — the Retention Dance:** cycle **Unique info → Normal info → Unique info**. Never stack the normal stuff; the moment it feels like you're out of new ideas, they leave
- **Outro — the Loop Trap:** don't ask for likes. Summarise the key points (relieves information overload), then create a *need* for the next video

## Escalating POV
*Source: Tim Danilov (faceless, 100M+ views)*

Story-driven and faceless narrative content.

Place the viewer in a role — "POV: you are the cartel's deadliest hitman" — then move
chronologically, with **each stage darker, more complex, and higher-stakes than the last**.
Never let stakes plateau; a flat middle is the churn point.

Built from **Script Bending**: unique idea + proven storytelling framework borrowed from a
competitor or adjacent niche.

## Hero's Journey for tutorials
*Source: Isaac*

How-to content that needs emotional pull, when a flat tutorial would lose people.

Status Quo → Call to Adventure → Assistance → Departure → Trials (wins, losses, mistakes) →
Approach (a deeper problem appears) → Crisis (lowest point) → Treasure (the realisation) →
Result → Return → New Life → Resolution.

The mechanism: run a **surface problem** (the technical how-to) *and* a **deeper problem**
(the emotional struggle — perfectionism, fear of posting) in parallel. The surface problem
delivers the value; the deeper problem creates the tension that carries the middle.

Compress heavily for short-form — often just Status Quo → Trials → Crisis → Treasure → New Life.

## Road Map structure
*Source: Abhimanyu Singh (Yash)*

Not a shape — a *method* for how to write any of the above.

1. **Topic structure** — where it starts, the middle points, where it ends
2. **Hard key facts** — dates, names, numbers, written down separately (memory drops these on camera)
3. **Examples / stories** — one or two, noted as prompts not prose

**Maximum 1–2 pages.** More than that and it's useless — it's a one-minute video.

Never write word-for-word: reading someone's writing (even your own) reads robotic, and the
camera catches it. Explain the topic to 4–5 friends first; your brain finds the natural human
pattern, and that flow is the script.
