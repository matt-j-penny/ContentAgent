# Hook Library

The first 1.5–3 seconds decide the video. Pick a type, then write three variants.

---

## The 9 hook types

### 1. Pattern Interrupt
A visual or audio element that doesn't match the surrounding feed — snaps the viewer out of autopilot scrolling. This is the "stop" half of **Stop → Stack**; on its own it buys attention but doesn't hold it.

### 2. The Call Out
Names the viewer's exact situation so they think *"wait, are they talking about me?"*
Runs on identity recognition + a curiosity gap. Must be specific enough to sting.

- ✅ "If your last five videos have under 500 views, you're stupid."
- ✅ "Take that food out of your mouth, you slob."
- ✅ "You've been eating this fruit your entire life and you have no idea."
- ❌ "Most creators make mistakes with their hooks." (vague, no sting, no gap)

Rule: **always "you", never "I"**. "I used to struggle with my hooks" puts the ball in the viewer's court and they swipe.

### 3. The Question Hook
A specific, answerable question the viewer actually cares about — then **pre-block their intuitive answer** to widen the gap.

- "Do you know why the most jacked guys at the gym are always the weakest? **And it has nothing to do with steroids.**"
- "Do you know why Mr Beast turned down $1 billion?"

Must close the loop by the end. An unanswered question hook tanks engagement even if watch time holds.

### 4. The Comparison Hook
Two things that don't belong together, or that contradict a common belief — creates visual *and* conceptual tension.

- "This video took me 7 hours to film and 20 seconds to edit."

### 5. The Negative Hook (loss aversion)
People are roughly **twice as motivated to avoid pain as to seek reward**. A warning outperforms the same advice framed positively.

- "The worst possible thing you could do for your personal brand is this."
- "Never ever ever post a video on Instagram without using this feature." (triple negative — deliberate)
- "This could ruin your life if you don't do it correctly."
- Softer variant: "You're making content way harder than it has to be."

### 6. The Anti-Hook
Start mid-action, no context, no setup. The brain fills the gap and stays to resolve it.

- "He jumped out as his plane was hit by enemy fire, but as he parachuted to the ground, they kept shooting."

### 7. Script Bending
Take a hook framework proven in another niche and adapt it — structure only, never the words.

### 8. Trend Hook
Attach to a person, event or clip already spreading. Borrowed validation buys the first second.

- "This girl is trending a lot on the internet, and you should learn a lesson from her…"

### 9. Result Hook
Open on the finished outcome, then rewind to the process. The plated meal before the cooking.

- "I'm going to the gym for the first time, come spend a day with me."
- "From eating raw cherries to contaminated water at the White House… this is how every US president died."

---

## Construction rules

**Stop → Stack.** Interrupt first, *then* stack meaning. A pattern interrupt with nothing stacked behind it gets a 1-second view.

**Layer three hooks simultaneously** — spoken + on-screen text + visual. Assume mute playback: the text hook must carry the promise alone.

**Two visual changes in the first 3 seconds.** Movement keeps the visual cortex engaged while the audio hook works.

**Click confirmation within 3–6 seconds.** Show or say the thing the title/thumbnail/first frame promised. This is the single most common retention leak in the 5–10s range.

**First-frame optimisation.** Videos often render before they play — the top half of frame one has to work as a still.

**Fast pace, no bloat.** No greetings, no "in today's video", no personal context. Long intro = bad hook, full stop.

---

## Why hooks fail

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Vague scenario, no emotion | Name the exact situation the viewer is in |
| Question nobody loses sleep over | Ask something they can't self-answer, then block the guess |
| Introduction bloat | Delete the first sentence. Usually the video starts at sentence two |
| Hook doesn't match the body | Rewrite the hook to the video you actually made — clickbait retention is worse than an honest weaker hook |
| Attracts the wrong crowd | A viral hook that pulls non-buyers is a failed hook |
| No curiosity gap | Give a reason to stay to the end — a promised reward |

## Additional hook lines

Openers, not finished hooks — each needs the specific topic dropped in immediately after (or, on withheld-subject lines, deliberately held back one more beat). Most are Call Out, Negative Hook, or Question Hook variants; a few are their own thing.

- **"No one talks about this…"** — Use when the angle is genuinely overlooked, not just under-covered by you personally. Curiosity gap + implied insider status. Fails if the "overlooked" fact is common knowledge — the viewer catches the bluff immediately.
- **"You're not going to like this but"** — Use when the value delivery is a warning or an uncomfortable truth. Pre-frames friction so the viewer braces rather than swipes when the bad news lands. Negative Hook family.
- **"Here's the truth about…"** — Use when correcting a popular misconception. Name the misconception in the same breath — on its own this line is an explainer lead-in (banned in Step 5) and reads as filler.
- **"This is why you are still…"** — Use when diagnosing a stuck state the viewer relates to ("why you're still doing X by hand"). Call Out + implied fix. Needs a specific, nameable habit, not a vague malaise.
- **"Never ever ever…"** — Use for one specific mistake, never a list — the triple negative is deliberate emphasis and dilutes fast if stacked with other warnings. Say the exact action in the next breath.
- **"If I had to start over…"** — Use when sharing a compressed, hard-won lesson or a redo of your own process. Implies authority through experience; works best as an Opinion/take opener.
- **"This will change how you think about AI"** — Use only when the video reframes a belief, not when it just adds a fact. High promise, high accountability — the payoff has to actually deliver a reframe or it reads as clickbait.
- **"I learnt this way too late and it cost me"** — Use when there's a real, specific cost (time, money, a missed opportunity). Loss aversion only works if the cost is named later, not left abstract.
- **"No one is going to give you permission, so I will"** — Use for permission-granting, manifesto-style Opinion pieces where the viewer is hesitating on something they already want to do.
- **"There's one thing about x that changes everything…"** — Withheld-subject hook. Name *x* immediately, hold back the "one thing" for the open loop. Works across New Tool, Tutorial, Comparison.
- **"This might trigger you but"** — Use only for content that's genuinely contrarian for *this* audience. Overusing it on safe opinions burns the "might trigger you" promise and readers stop believing the warning.
- **"Nobody wants to admit this but"** — Same mechanism as "no one talks about this," with an added implication of denial/shame. Use when the target belief is one people avoid saying out loud, not just one they haven't heard.
- **"This might offend you but…"** — Same caution as "might trigger you." Reserve for claims with real disagreement attached; don't spend it on mild takes.
- **"If you feel behind with AI right now, good you should feel behind"** — Use to reframe a common anxiety as reasonable rather than something to fix. Call Out + permission; strong opener for pace-of-change Opinion pieces.
- **"I hate to be the bearer of bad news"** (+ topic) — Use for genuinely negative news (price hikes, feature removal, shutdowns, layoffs). State the topic in the same sentence — don't let this line stand alone as its own beat.
- **"How does x do y"** (something amazing) — Question Hook. Use only when the capability is demonstrable and genuinely surprising; must be answered inside the video, not left open.
- **"Most people don't know"** (+ topic) — Use for a widely-available feature or fact that's underused. Flatters the viewer into feeling ahead of the crowd — needs the topic named immediately, same as "here's the truth about."
- **"Nobody mentions this"** — Identical use case to "no one talks about this" / "nobody wants to admit this" — pick one per script, don't stack synonyms across a single video (breaks the "one word per thing" rule).
- **"I wish I knew this sooner"** — Softer version of "cost me" — regret without a quantified cost. Use for smaller tips (a shortcut, a setting) rather than big stakes.
- **"Ever notice this pattern.."** — Recognition hook: points at something the viewer has half-noticed but never named. Use when you can actually name the pattern in the next line — vague pattern-hooks with no payoff read as filler.
- **"Let me save you hours"** — Direct value promise for Tutorial content. Pairs naturally with a Result Hook shape — show the time saved, then rewind to how.
- **"This may surprise you"** — Use only when the fact that follows genuinely contradicts expectation. Generic on its own; the surprise has to land in the very next line or the hook reads as a stalling phrase.
- **"I just figured this out"** — Implies freshness and first-hand discovery. Pair with a recency marker ("just," "today") so it reads as news, not an old realization.
- **"The absolutely worst thing you can do right now…"** — Negative Hook. Use for one specific, currently-relevant mistake — "right now" needs to be true, not decorative.
- **"Disclaimer, this is going to be different to any other video you've seen on this topic"** — High bar: only use when the take genuinely diverges from the saturated coverage of a topic. If it isn't actually different, this line makes the letdown worse, not better.
- **"X just did y but z"** (Google just cloned Lovable but made it free) — Comparison Hook baked into the line itself. Use for launches/news with a twist worth surfacing in the hook rather than saving for the body.
- **"You can now do x and this may have just z"** — Use for a new capability with a real consequence (good or bad). Needs the recency marker "now" to read as news rather than a standing fact.
- **"Someone just did x"** — Withheld-subject hook. Use when naming *who* immediately would kill the curiosity gap — good for drama/news where the reveal is part of the reward.
- **"This is the exact framework you need to become a x"** — Use for a genuinely repeatable process with a named outcome/identity attached ("become a x"). Overpromising a "framework" for a single loose tip reads as bait.
- **"Your vibe coded apps are going to get hacked so fast"** — Niche-specific warning (AI coding audience). Use only with credible evidence to back it in the value delivery — an unsupported scare claim in this niche gets called out fast.
- **"Things you didn't know you could do using x"** — Use for multiple underused features of a known tool — this is the numbered-list body shape (two to three items, counted out loud), not a single-fact video.
- **"This is the one app you need to download this year"** — Strong singular recommendation. Only earns the "the one" framing when there's a real comparison behind it — otherwise it reads as an ad.
- **"If you're still doing x then y"** — Call Out + consequence. Use for an outdated method with a real, specific downside — "y" has to be a genuine cost, not a vague scold.
- **"Y just destroyed X but doing Z"** — Use for a real competitive dethroning (a Comparison or News story), where Z is the specific, non-obvious detail that makes it worth watching.
- **"Stop doing x"** — Negative Hook + Call Out. Use for one specific bad habit, and follow immediately with the reason — "stop doing x" alone with no payoff is the emptiest version of this line.

## Which hooks fit which content type

Maps the hook types and lines above onto the 5 content types in `references/content-types.md`. Not one-to-one — most hooks work for more than one type; use judgement on the specific script.

**New model / new tool** — Question Hook, Comparison Hook, Result Hook, Trend Hook (if the launch is already trending).
"This will change how you think about AI" · "How does x do y" · "This is the one app you need to download this year" · "X just did y but z" · "You can now do x and this may have just z" · "Someone just did x" · "Things you didn't know you could do using x" · "There's one thing about x that changes everything…" · "This may surprise you"

**News story** — Trend Hook, Anti-Hook (drop into the story mid-action), Negative Hook, Question Hook.
"I hate to be the bearer of bad news" (+ topic) · "Someone just did x" · "X just did y but z" · "No one talks about this…" · "Nobody mentions this" · "Your vibe coded apps are going to get hacked so fast" · "This may surprise you"

**Tutorial / process** — Result Hook (show the outcome, rewind to steps), The Call Out, Negative Hook.
"Let me save you hours" · "This is the exact framework you need to become a x" · "Never ever ever…" · "Stop doing x" · "If you're still doing x then y" · "The absolutely worst thing you can do right now…" · "I wish I knew this sooner" · "I learnt this way too late and it cost me" · "Most people don't know" (+ topic) · "Things you didn't know you could do using x"

**Opinion / take** — The Call Out, Negative Hook, Question Hook.
"You're not going to like this but" · "Here's the truth about…" · "Nobody wants to admit this but" · "This might trigger you but" · "This might offend you but…" · "No one is going to give you permission, so I will" · "If you feel behind with AI right now, good you should feel behind" · "I just figured this out" · "Ever notice this pattern.." · "Disclaimer, this is going to be different…" · "If I had to start over…" · "This is why you are still…"

**Comparison** — Comparison Hook, Question Hook.
"X just did y but z" · "Y just destroyed X but doing Z" · "There's one thing about x that changes everything…" · "This may surprise you" · "This video took me 7 hours to film and 20 seconds to edit" (proven-13 example of the Comparison Hook shape)

**Type-agnostic** — Pattern Interrupt and Script Bending are execution/structure techniques, not content-dependent. They layer on top of whichever type you've picked rather than pointing to one.
