# What makes a good title

Read by `titles` and `packager`. The title and thumbnail are one unit: the thumbnail grabs the eye, the title closes the click. Neither should repeat the other.

## The job

A title has to do three things in about a second:
1. **Tell them what it's about**, clearly enough that the right person knows it's for them.
2. **Make them want it**: a result, a curiosity gap, stakes, or a strong opinion.
3. **Be believable**: specific, and something the video actually delivers.

If the video can't deliver the title, the title is wrong. Clicks that leave in 30 seconds hurt more than clicks that never happened.

## What works

- **Specific beats vague.** "I tried 7 AI note takers" beats "AI note takers compared". Numbers, names, timeframes and money are specific.
- **A clear result or transformation.** What the viewer gets or becomes. "…in 10 minutes", "…without coding".
- **A curiosity gap**, opened but not closed: something they need to watch to resolve. "The setting that made my videos look expensive." Close it in the video, early enough.
- **Stakes or a threat.** What they lose or get wrong if they don't watch. "You're using ChatGPT wrong" works when it's true for the audience.
- **Contrast.** Expectation vs. reality, cheap vs. expensive, old vs. new, beginner vs. pro.
- **The viewer in it.** "You" and "your" when it fits naturally.
- **Plain words.** The words the audience would search or say, not jargon or brand codenames.
- **Front-load the hook.** The first 3-5 words carry it; mobile and search cut titles off.

## Length and form

- Aim for under 60 characters, ideally 40-55. Never rely on anything after ~70.
- Title case or sentence case, matching the user's channel. No all caps except one word for emphasis, if the niche does that.
- At most one bracket or colon. Emojis and hashtags rarely help on long form.
- No clickbait the video doesn't pay off, no misleading numbers.

## Patterns

Proven shapes (adapt; never copy a title word for word). `X` and `Y` are the topic.

| Pattern | Example shape |
|---|---|
| Result + method | "How I [result] with [method]" |
| Number + promise | "7 [things] that [result]" |
| Mistake / warning | "Stop doing [X]" · "You're doing [X] wrong" |
| Tested / experiment | "I tried [X] for 30 days" · "I tested every [X]" |
| Comparison / verdict | "[X] vs [Y]: which is actually better?" |
| Secret / hidden | "The [X] nobody talks about" |
| Before / after | "From [bad state] to [good state] in [time]" |
| Contrarian | "Why I stopped [common advice]" |
| Ultimate guide | "[X] explained in [short time]" · "The only [X] guide you need" |
| News | "[Thing] just changed [what the viewer cares about]" |

The Vault (`list_vault_titles`) holds many more proven titles; read it before writing.

## Pairing with the thumbnail

- The thumbnail shows the emotion, the result or the "thing"; the title says what it means.
- Thumbnail text (if any) adds to the title, never repeats it. Title "I tried 7 AI note takers", thumbnail text "only 1 worked".
- Together they should open one clear question.

## Scoring a title (1-5 each)

| Criterion | Ask |
|---|---|
| Clarity | Would the target viewer know what this is in one glance? |
| Desire | Do they want the result or need to resolve the gap? |
| Specificity | Is there a number, name, timeframe or concrete outcome? |
| Believability | Will the video clearly deliver it? |
| Fit | Does it sound like the channel and suit the audience's words? |
| Pairing | Does it work with the thumbnail without repeating it? |

Also check: under 60 characters, hook in the first five words. Rank by total; break ties with Desire.

## Instagram and Shorts

Short-form "titles" are the on-screen hook text and the caption's first line:
- **On-screen hook text:** 3-8 words, readable in under a second, matches or sharpens the spoken hook. Uses the hook patterns in `hooks.md`.
- **Caption first line:** what shows before "more". Restate the promise or add the missing piece; put a keyword or CTA after.
- YouTube Shorts titles: short, keyword-led, under 40 characters.

## Niche patterns

Titles vary a lot by niche. Before writing, look at 10-20 recent outliers in the user's niche (vidIQ or the competitors' channels): which patterns repeat, how long they are, what casing and punctuation they use. Follow the niche's conventions unless breaking one is the point.
