# What makes a good thumbnail

Read by `thumbnails` and `packager`. A thumbnail is seen at about the size of a stamp, for under a second, next to a dozen others. It has to win that.

## The rules

- **One focal point.** One face, one object or one result. If the eye doesn't know where to land, it moves on.
- **Readable small.** Check at 160×90 pixels. If the idea is lost, simplify.
- **A face with real emotion** usually helps when the creator is the brand: surprise, concern, curiosity, delight. The expression should match the video's feeling. Eyes toward the focal object pull attention to it.
- **Contrast.** Bright subject on a darker or plain background; colours that stand out from YouTube's white and dark modes. Avoid busy backgrounds.
- **Minimal text.** 0-4 words, big, high contrast. Text adds what the title doesn't say. Never repeat the title.
- **Show the result or the stakes,** not the process: the after, the number, the broken thing, the before/after.
- **Leave the bottom right clear**: the video length covers it.
- **Consistent but not identical.** A recognisable style across the channel, but each thumbnail must stand out from the user's own recent ones.

## Concept types

| Type | What it shows | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Reaction | Face with strong emotion beside the thing | Reviews, tests, news |
| Before / after | Split frame, bad vs good | Transformations, tutorials |
| The object | One product, tool or screen, huge | Reviews, "this one thing" |
| The result | A number, a graph going up, money, a finished build | Case studies, experiments |
| Versus | Two things facing off | Comparisons |
| Mystery | Something blurred, circled or covered | Secrets, curiosity titles |
| Big text | 2-3 punchy words carry it | Opinion, warnings (use sparingly) |
| Scene | The creator in a striking setting or situation | Stories, challenges, vlogs |

## Pairing with the title

- Together they open one question; each carries half.
- Title says what it is, thumbnail shows why it matters (or the reverse).
- Thumbnail text and title never share words.

## The concept brief

Every thumbnail concept is written as a brief someone could shoot or generate:

- **Concept type** (from the table)
- **Focal point:** what the eye hits first
- **Composition:** what's where (left/right/centre), framing (close-up, medium)
- **Face and expression** (if any): what the emotion is and why
- **Text on it:** exact words, or none
- **Colours and background**
- **Why it works with the title**

For the Packaging `description` field, condense to one or two sentences: what's in it, the text, the emotion.

## Generating images

When an image generator is connected:
- Generate the background, object or scene; the creator's real face is better photographed than generated. If the user wants their face, ask for a photo or leave a clear spot for one.
- Never generate a real, identifiable person other than the user, and only the user with their consent and their own reference photo.
- 16:9, 1280×720 or larger. Generate 2-4 variants per concept.
- Add text afterwards in an editor rather than relying on the generator to spell it, unless the generator handles text well.
- Check each at small size before showing it.

## Scoring (1-5 each)

| Criterion | Ask |
|---|---|
| Focus | Is there one obvious focal point? |
| Small-size readability | Still clear at 160×90? |
| Emotion / intrigue | Does it make you feel or wonder something? |
| Contrast | Does it stand out in a feed, in light and dark mode? |
| Pairing | Does it complete the title without repeating it? |
| Honesty | Does the video deliver what it shows? |

## Shorts and Reels covers

- Vertical 9:16 cover, but the grid crops Instagram covers to about 3:4 (4:5 in some views): keep the subject and text in the centre.
- The cover's job on the profile grid is to say the topic in 2-5 words; most short-form views come from the feed, not the cover.
